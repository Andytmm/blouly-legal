Privacy Policy

Last updated 21 August 2026 · All legal documents

Blouly is a small app made by one person. It has no advertising, no analytics, and no tracking of any kind. This policy explains the data it does handle — what stays on your phone, what is stored in the cloud, and the one thing that gets sent to an AI provider to write your cards.

The short version. We store your email address, the topic names you type, and the cards generated for you. To write a card, the topic name you wrote is sent to Anthropic's API. Nothing that identifies you is sent with it. We do not sell or share your data, we run no advertising or analytics, and you can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app at any time.

1. Who we are

Blouly is provided by Andrei Safronov, an individual established in the United States ("we", "us"). For data protection purposes we are the controller of the personal data described in this policy.

You can reach us at privacy@blouly.com. This policy covers the Blouly iOS app and the backend service it talks to. It does not cover the other apps you use Blouly alongside — those have their own policies.

2. What we collect and why

Subscription data

If you subscribe, Apple takes the payment and we never see your card details, your billing address or your invoices — Apple does not make any of that available to apps, which is why the app links you to your iPhone's own subscription settings to manage or cancel.

What we do hold is a record that your account is subscribed: which plan, when it expires, whether it is set to renew, and whether you are in the free trial. We keep it so that our server can check you are entitled before it writes you a card, and so that your subscription works on any phone you sign in on. It is written by RevenueCat, which verifies the purchase with Apple on our behalf — see section 7. RevenueCat receives your Blouly account identifier and your App Store purchase record. It never receives your topics or your cards.

Technical data

When the app asks our server for a card, it sends your device's time zone (for example Europe/Berlin). We use it for one thing: working out when your day rolls over, so the daily card allowance resets at midnight where you are rather than midnight UTC. It is not stored against your account and is not used to locate you.

Like any internet service, our hosting provider records standard server request logs, which include IP addresses, as part of operating and securing the service. We do not use those logs to build a profile of you.

Separately, when something goes wrong on our server we record the failure itself: which part of the service failed, a technical error message, and — where the request had signed you in — your account identifier, so we can tell whether a fault affected one person or everyone. These records are deleted after 30 days, and they are removed immediately if you delete your account. They never contain your topics or your cards. That is a deliberate rule and not a side effect: what you type is the one thing in this app we would least want copied into an operations log.

We also keep a simple count of how much work our AI provider has done for us: one line per card or suggestion written, recording when and which of the two, linked to your account while the account exists. We use it for one thing — knowing what the service costs to run, and being warned if that jumps unexpectedly. It records no topic, no card text and no device information. If you delete your account, the link to you is erased and what remains is an anonymous tally that says only that a card was written at a particular time.

3. What never leaves your device

Some of what Blouly knows about you is deliberately kept on your phone and is never transmitted to us or anyone else. We built it this way on purpose, because this is the most sensitive data the app touches.

  • Your blocked-app list — which apps you have chosen to be interrupted on, and whether each is switched on.
  • Your interruption history — how many times today you have opened each of those apps. This is behavioural data about your habits, and it stays on your phone.
  • Grace timers — the short windows after you tap "Open app" during which Blouly leaves you alone.
  • Your daily goal — your target, today's count, and today's date.
  • Your sign-in tokens — held in the iOS Keychain and marked so that they cannot be restored onto a different device from an iCloud backup.

Because this data is only on your device, we cannot retrieve it, and it is destroyed when you delete the app.

4. What we do not collect

Stated plainly, because it is unusual enough to be worth saying:

  • No analytics and no crash reporting. There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporting SDK, and no product-usage telemetry in the app.
  • No advertising and no tracking. We do not collect an advertising identifier (IDFA), we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and the app never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt because it has nothing to ask for.
  • One third-party SDK, and only one. The app contains RevenueCat's library, which exists solely to make subscriptions work across your devices — see section 7. Everything else is built on Apple's own frameworks. There is no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or attribution SDK of any kind.
  • No location, contacts, photos, microphone, camera, health or fitness data. The app requests none of these permissions.
  • No selling or sharing. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

One thing worth disclosing. So that the apps-block feature can offer to interrupt the apps you actually have, Blouly is able to check whether ten named apps are installed on your phone: Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, X (Twitter), Reddit, Snapchat, Pinterest, LinkedIn and Twitch. That check happens entirely on your device, the result is never sent to us, and we cannot see which apps you have.

5. How your topics are used to write cards

Blouly's cards are written by an AI model — Claude, made by Anthropic. This is the only place your content leaves our own systems, so here is precisely what happens.

When you swipe for a new card, our server sends Anthropic's API:

  • the name of the topic you typed, and
  • up to 40 short summaries of cards you have already seen on that topic, so the model does not repeat itself.

When the app suggests a new topic to you, it sends up to 50 of your topic names so the suggestion fits your interests.

That is the entire payload. Your email address, your name, your account identifier, and any device identifier are not sent to Anthropic — they never form part of the request. Your device never contacts Anthropic directly; only our server does, using our own credentials.

Under our commercial agreement with Anthropic:

  • Anthropic does not train its models on the content we send through the API.
  • Anthropic automatically deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days of receiving or generating them.
  • The exception is content flagged under Anthropic's usage policies, which it may retain for up to 2 years, with associated safety classification scores kept for up to 7 years.

Please think before you type a topic. A topic name is free text, and it is the one piece of your content that leaves our systems. "Roman history" and "how sourdough works" are exactly what the app is for. Something like a medical diagnosis, a legal matter, or another person's private business is best kept out of it — not because we would misuse it, but because it would be sent to a third party for processing, and because it would be stored until you delete it.

Cards are generated by an AI model and can be wrong. They are general interest reading, not professional advice. See the Terms of Use.

7. Who else processes your data

We use a small number of providers to run the service. Each one is bound by a contract that requires it to protect your data to a standard equivalent to the one described in this policy, to process it only on our instructions, and to keep it confidential.

The full list, with what each one receives, is on the subprocessors page. In summary: Supabase hosts our database, accounts and server functions; Anthropic generates the card text; Apple handles Sign in with Apple and all subscription payments; RevenueCat verifies those subscriptions with Apple and tells our server you are entitled; and Google is involved only if you choose to sign in with a Google account.

Beyond these, we share your personal data only where we are legally required to — for example in response to a valid legal request — or where it is necessary to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. If Blouly is ever transferred to someone else, we will tell you before your data moves, and this policy will continue to apply until you are given a new one.

8. Where your data is stored

Your account, topics and cards are stored in the United States — specifically in our Supabase project hosted in the AWS us-west-2 region (Oregon). Anthropic processes card-generation requests in the United States, and RevenueCat processes subscription records there too. Apple and Google operate globally.

If you are in the UK, EU or EEA, this means your personal data is transferred outside your country. Those transfers are made under the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (and the UK Addendum where applicable), which our providers incorporate into their data processing terms, together with the technical measures described in section 12.

We do not otherwise put an expiry on your topics and cards, because the point of a saved card is that it is still there when you come back. If you want them gone sooner, delete the topic or the account.

Why deleting a topic takes 30 days to complete on our side. The topic and its cards leave the app immediately — you will not see them again, and we do not use them for anything after that point. We hold the rows for up to 30 days for two reasons: so that a topic deleted by mistake can be recovered if you ask us, and so that cards already written that day still count towards your daily limit. Without the second one, deleting a topic would hand back allowance you had already used. After 30 days the rows are deleted permanently by an automatic job.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app: Menu → Account → Delete account. It is immediate and it cannot be undone. Deleting the account removes the account itself, your profile, every topic and every card, in a single operation — there is no "deactivated" state and nothing is kept back. That includes anything still inside the 30-day window described above: the account deletion takes it all at once, and does not wait. You do not need to email us and we will not ask you why.

Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription. Apple owns the subscription, not us, and no app is able to cancel one on your behalf. If you are subscribed, cancel it first in Settings → your name → Subscriptions on your iPhone — otherwise Apple will keep billing you after your Blouly data is gone.

11. Children

Blouly is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 13, or under the higher minimum age set by your country where one applies (16 in some EU member states). We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has given us personal data, email us and we will delete it.

12. Security

No system is perfectly secure, but here is what we actually do:

  • All traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted in transit with HTTPS.
  • Every request to our backend carries your own signed session token. There is no shared password or shared key built into the app — if there were, extracting it from the app would give someone access to everyone's data.
  • The database enforces row-level security: each row is tied to a user, and a request authenticated as one user cannot read or modify another user's rows.
  • Our AI provider credentials live only on the server, never in the app.
  • Your sign-in tokens are stored in the iOS Keychain and marked so they cannot travel to another device via an iCloud backup.
